    About this map

    On 15 and 16 April 2024 a slow-moving storm brought the United Arab Emirates its heaviest rain since records began in 1949. The National Center of Meteorology measured 254.8 mm in under 24 hours at Khatm Al Shakla, near Al Ain: more than the country usually gets in a year. In Dubai, main roads such as Sheikh Zayed Road flooded, Dubai International Airport cancelled and diverted hundreds of flights, Metro stations closed, and some homes were cut off for days. People died in the UAE and in neighbouring Oman.

    In this web experiment, the storm is a case study of two ways of mapping weather: as visualisation and as simulation. Both are based on historical data from the ERA5 dataset, ECMWF's hour-by-hour reconstruction of the world's past weather (the visualisation adds the visibility and the weather in words from the reports of Dubai International Airport). These two modes of mapping reflect the dual nature of the ERA5 dataset itself: at once a product of observations using realtime sensors, with gaps filled in using a physics-based simulation. The result is a dataset – and a map – that stakes an epistemological claim via the combination of both empirical data and mathematical formulae.

    Visualisation. The panel shows the wind, rain and cloud cover from ERA5 (reanalysed) and the visibility reported at Dubai International Airport (observed); the symbols over the city show the same weather in 3D, and the sun lights the buildings as it did at that hour.

    Simulation. How the storm's wind moved between the buildings. Around each place, a circle of the city 800 m across holds a simulation of the air at street level, seen from straight above: the wind comes in from the direction ERA5 gives for that hour, and yellow streaks follow it round the buildings, squeezed and faster between towers, slow and turning behind them. Modelled, indicative only: the simulation is flat, with no building heights, and its air is slower and smoother than the storm's, so it shows the patterns of the flow, not real speeds or gusts. Every place gets the same ERA5 wind, from one grid cell about 28 km across, which smooths out the storm's gusts and squalls.

    Sources and limits

    • Buildings: Cesium OSM Buildings, made by Cesium from OpenStreetMap data, © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL. Streamed from Cesium ion, not stored here. Building heights in OpenStreetMap are incomplete and partly estimated, and some buildings are plain blocks (the Burj Al Arab has no sail). Cartographic.
    • Satellite imagery: the default imagery from Cesium ion (credited on the globe), in its own colours. Cartographic.
    • Visibility and the weather in words ("heavy thunderstorm with rain"): the 100 METAR reports from Dubai International Airport (OMDB, 25.25° N, 55.36° E) between 15 April 00:00 and 17 April 00:00 2024, Dubai time, from the Iowa Environmental Mesonet ASOS/METAR archive, Iowa State University (mesonet.agron.iastate.edu), downloaded 2026-11-16. Observed. The reports also give the wind, which the map does not use (it uses ERA5's, below), and no rain amounts. One report is used for the whole map.
    • Rain (mm in the hour), cloud cover (low, mid, high, %) and wind 10 m above the ground (km/h and direction, at the top of each hour; each moment takes the nearest hour), for the symbols, the panel and the simulation: ERA5 hourly reanalysis by ECMWF for the Copernicus Climate Change Service, through the Open-Meteo historical weather API (CC BY 4.0), at the ERA5 grid cell at 25.25° N, 55.25° E (about 28 km across), downloaded 2026-11-16; wind added 2026-11-27. Reanalysed: an estimate, not a rain-gauge reading. Contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information 2024. ERA5 smooths storms like this one over its grid, and its heaviest rain comes later than the airport's. Its wind has no gusts and is often weaker than the airport's measured wind, and from another direction (at 15:11 on 16 April the airport reported 44 km/h from the north-west; ERA5 has 29 km/h from the east-north-east).
    • Building outlines for the simulation: OpenStreetMap, © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0, downloaded through the Overpass API by tools/fetch_footprints.mjs (data/footprints.json): a square 800 m across around each place, outlines only, no heights. Cartographic. Buildings missing from OpenStreetMap are missing from the simulation.
    • Airflow: simulated in the page by js/airflow.js, a lattice Boltzmann model (D2Q9, BGK) on a 200 × 200 grid of 4 m cells, with the buildings as solid walls and the ERA5 wind 10 m above the ground at the nearest hour to each moment (reanalysed) as the incoming flow. Modelled, indicative: a flat slice with no heights, a much slower and smoother flow than the storm (low Reynolds number), and one wind for every place. Speeds are scaled only to show stronger and weaker wind; they are not real speeds.
