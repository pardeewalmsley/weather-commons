    The damage

    On 15 and 16 April 2024 the United Arab Emirates had its heaviest rain since records began in 1949. The National Center of Meteorology (NCM) measured 254.8 mm in under 24 hours at Khatm Al Shakla near Al Ain, more than the country usually gets in a year. In Dubai, Sheikh Zayed Road and other main roads flooded, Metro stations closed, and many homes were cut off or left without power and water for days. Dubai International Airport, one of the world's busiest, diverted and cancelled hundreds of flights, and it took days to recover. People died in the UAE and in neighbouring Oman, where flash floods struck a day earlier.

    Conspiracy theories

    Within hours, posts on social media blamed the UAE's cloud-seeding programme for the floods: that seeding had gone wrong, or that the weather had been changed on purpose. Early news reports added to this by quoting a forecaster who said seeding flights had flown in the days before the storm; the NCM later said that no seeding took place during the storm itself. The claims spread far beyond the region and joined older theories about weather control and geoengineering.

    Meteorologists answered that seeding cannot make a storm of this size: at best it adds a little rain to clouds that are already able to rain, and this storm was a large weather system that forecast models had seen coming days ahead. Attribution scientists pointed instead to a warming climate, which makes heavy rain of this kind heavier, though the data for the region were too few to say by exactly how much.

    The UAE cloud-seeding program

    The UAE has seeded clouds since the 1990s, now through the NCM. Planes fly into growing convective clouds, often over the Hajar Mountains in the east, and fire flares of salt particles that help water droplets grow large enough to fall. Since 2015 the UAE Research Program for Rain Enhancement Science has funded research into it around the world. The country gets very little rain and draws most of its drinking water from the sea through desalination; seeding is one of the ways it tries to add to its water. How much rain seeding really adds is still debated among scientists.
